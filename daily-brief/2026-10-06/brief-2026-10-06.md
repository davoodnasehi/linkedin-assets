## 1. Modern consultative selling — what separates winners in complex B2B (the RAIN Group / insight-selling evidence)

**The insight.** RAIN Group analysed **731 B2B purchases** by buyers representing **US$3.1B in annual purchasing power** across 42 factors. The three factors that most separate winners from second-place finishers were, in order: **(1) "Educated me with new ideas and perspectives", (2) "Collaborated with me", (3) "Persuaded me we would achieve results."** Two counter-intuitive findings matter:
- **"Deepened my understanding of my needs" ranked 40th of 42** in what winners *do* — yet "understood my needs" was the **5th-largest gap** versus second-place finishers. Translation: buyers assume competence; they do not reward a seller merely for restating the problem back to them.
- **Differentiation factors dominated the top of the list** — three of the top four of 42. In complex sales, being *clearly distinct* matters more than being *thorough*.

The operative model is **Connect → Convince → Collaborate**. "Educate" splits into **opportunity insight** (the buyer should be doing something they don't know about yet) and **interaction insight** (disruptive questions that push the buyer out of the status quo). Trust = depend on them, listen to them, give them access, spend time with them.

**Application to S5.**
- The §3-A selling narrative should **lead with an idea the buyer doesn't have**: that IEC 61511 proof-test intervals are *calculated, not defaulted* (PFDavg ≈ λDU × TI / 2), and that continuous condition-monitoring data can legitimately extend a validated interval — an "opportunity insight" a reliability engineer will not have heard from a hardware vendor.
- The winning posture is **collaborator, not presenter** — the fixed-scope "AI readiness / FS gap assessment" is structurally a collaboration (the buyer's team supplies criticality data and CMMS history; S5 supplies the diagnostic-coverage argument). Sell the *joint working session*, not a deck.
- **Do not over-index on "understanding their needs."** Buyers already assume you can. Differentiate: CFSE/NER credential + IEC 61511 DC/proof-test methodology + audit-traceable records — the exact "new idea + new perspective" axis. This also tells the org how to write the offer page and the AEO articles (§3-L/§3-M): lead with the insight, not the spec sheet.

*Sources:* RAIN Group, *What Sales Winners Do Differently* (731 B2B purchases, US$3.1B buyer purchasing power, 42 factors); RAIN Group, *The Future of Consultative Selling*; Trusted Advisor *Insight Selling* Q&A (Mike Schultz).

## 2. Buyer enablement & the B2B buying journey — where content actually does the selling (Gartner evidence)

**The insight.** Gartner's Digital B2B Buyer Survey (n = 750) found buyers spend only **17% of the purchase journey meeting potential suppliers** — and that 17% is split across every vendor, leaving any single firm **~5–6% of the buyer's total decision time**. Buyers identify **~89% of business problems without supplier input**, engage vendors roughly **57% of the way through** the journey, and loop non-linearly through **six buying jobs**: problem identification → solution exploration → requirements building → supplier selection → validation → consensus creation. **~90% of buyers loop back to at least one job.**

Gartner's answer is **buyer enablement**: information engineered to complete those buying jobs, in two forms — *prescriptive advice* ("do this / don't do this") and *practical support* (tools that complete a discrete job). Customers who engage with buyer enablement content are **3× more likely to make a high-quality purchase** (a more ambitious solution, at a premium price).

**Application to S5.**
- S5's content strategy (§3-H awareness engine, §3-L AEO layer, §3-M content-competitor watch) is a **buyer-enablement play, not an SEO play**. The distinction matters for what gets written.
- **Practical support is the missing asset.** S5's differentiator should be a downloadable *tool*, not just an article: e.g. a **"Proof-test interval & diagnostic-coverage worksheet"** (λDU inputs → PFDavg → max permissible TI) and a **"Condition-monitoring evidence pack checklist"** mapped to WA MSMS / registered-plant record duties. These complete buying jobs (requirements building, validation) where Factory AI et al. only publish comparison tables.
- **Prescriptive advice** for S5: "Do not select a condition-monitoring vendor before your criticality ranking exists" (the confirmed deal-killer) and "Do not accept a 'SIL-rated sensor' claim — SIL attaches to a function, not a product." Both are opinionated, credible (CFSE/NER), and nobody in the Ai-PdM class writes them.
- **Consensus creation is the real battleground:** the buying group averages ~11 people and the funded owner + technical lead + superintendent must agree. Ship a one-page "internal business case" template the buyer can forward — that is buyer enablement that travels through the org for you.

*Sources:* Gartner, Digital B2B Buyer Survey (n = 750) / *Win More B2B Sales Deals*; Gartner, "Marketing's Role in Buyer Enablement"; Gartner buyer-enablement research (six buying jobs, prescriptive advice + practical support).

## 3. Pricing productized engineering services — when value-based pricing works, and when it destroys the margin

**The insight.** The 2025–26 data is a reality check on value-based pricing (VBP). Promethean Research's *2026 State of Digital Services Report* shows **VBP usage fell from 31% of firms (2024) to 18% (2025)** — and the firms still using it **grew more slowly than firms on time-and-materials, fixed-fee or retainer models**. VBP "falls apart the moment you can't measure or attribute the result." The rule: VBP needs **three boxes ticked — a measurable outcome, a client who will share the numbers, and your ability to attribute the result.** Miss any one and you should charge a **scoped fixed fee** or a **hybrid retainer**. Where VBP does apply, the slice is **10–30% of first-year value** for revenue work, or **1–2× the annual cost removed** for cost-reduction work. Meanwhile **fixed-fee / productized work produces the best margin (45–65% gross)** for solo and small-team operators because it rewards efficiency, and **retainers produce the best revenue predictability**. Professional-services peers advise: **fixed-fee where the scope is well-defined; time-based where it isn't; retainer for ongoing advisory.**

**Application to S5.**
- The A$4,900 validation pilot is correctly priced as a **market-entry fixed fee** — keep it. But the *scale* offer should be **fixed-fee productized, not VBP**: S5 cannot yet attribute a client's avoided-downtime saving precisely enough to charge a percentage, and a mining buyer will not share reliability-cost numbers early. Fixed-fee per deliverable (SIS-boundary map + DC argument + IEC 61511 review pack) is the defensible model.
- **Guard the scope.** Scope creep is the #1 killer of productized offers. Mitigate with (a) a precise, named deliverable; (b) a standardized intake questionnaire + fixed data-request list; (c) milestone payment (e.g. 30/40/30 — kickoff / delivery / acceptance); (d) an explicit "out of scope" line in the SOW.
- **Budget for the cost-shock.** The first engagements cost more in owner/engineering hours than they return — the literature is explicit. Price and resource the first two pilots knowing they are loss-leaders for the evidence pack.
- **Retainer tier comes after G2/G3**, not before: land with the fixed-scope assessment → expand into recurring advisory (quarterly FS gap review, annual compliance update, PMHMP trigger-level review). That is the bow-tie (land-and-expand) sequence, and it de-correlates revenue from the mining capex cycle (T2).

*Sources:* Promethean Research, *2026 State of Digital Services Report* (VBP usage 31%→18%); SoDA/Productive pricing survey; Consulting Success (value-based gross-margin data); Ontario PEO *Fee Guidelines* (fixed-fee vs time-based vs retainer decision rule); productized-service literature (scope control, milestone pricing).

## 4. Customer validation, done properly in B2B — the Mom Test meets Jobs-to-be-Done and the buying committee

**The insight.** Rob Fitzpatrick's *The Mom Test* gives three rules: **talk about their life, not your idea; ask about specifics in the past, not hypotheticals; talk less than they do.** The single most valuable question is **"walk me through the last time this problem came up — what did you do, and what did it cost you?"** Asking about *past behaviour* is the only way to separate real signal from politeness. Layer **Clayton Christensen's Jobs-to-be-Done**: instead of "what features do you want?", ask **"what were you trying to accomplish when you started looking for a solution like this?"** For B2B specifically, the Mom Test is necessary but not sufficient: you must **map the buying committee before the first interview** (economic buyer, technical evaluator, end user, blocker), **triangulate evidence across roles**, and **count only costly signals** (a signed LOI with a deposit, a paid pilot, time committed) — never stated enthusiasm.

**Application to S5.**
- The validation machine's discovery calls should run strictly on Mom-Test questions, and the org should **pre-load the buying-committee map** for every target site (maintenance/asset manager = economic buyer; reliability lead = technical evaluator; superintendent = sign-off gate). This is org-side work; Dav only joins the call.
- **Redefine what counts as a gate signal.** Per this literature, a "would buy" is worth nothing; the G1/G2 gates are already correctly built on costly signals (magnet leads → discovery requests → signed scopes → paid pilots at ≥95% uptime). Hold that line.
- **The deal-killer is a validation finding, not an objection to overcome:** field_engineer confirmed a site with *no CMMS work-order history* must fix data capture first. The discovery call should *test for* that up front (ask "show me your last three condition-based work orders") so the pilot is scoped only where the evidence exists.
- **Triangulate:** one enthusiastic reliability engineer is not a validated segment. Require ≥2 independent sites confirming the same job-to-be-done before the offer page is considered validated.

*Sources:* Rob Fitzpatrick, *The Mom Test* (2013); Clayton M. Christensen et al., "Know Your Customers' Jobs to Be Done," *Harvard Business Review* (Sep 2016) and *Competing Against Luck* (2016); B2B customer-discovery practitioner literature (buying-committee mapping, evidence triangulation, costly signals).

## 5. Business development via accelerators — the honest evidence on equity vs customer access

**The insight.** The academic and practitioner literature converges on one evaluation criterion for accelerators: **whether the program provides access to the *right* customers and commercial partners** — that is the benefit most valuable to a technical founder, and the one most likely to justify the equity given up. Structurally, most accelerators take **4–8% equity** (Cohen & Hochberg) — "powered-by" programs like Plug and Play take **4–10%** — in exchange for pre-seed funding (typically US$25–100k), mentorship and network. **Equity dilution is the central cost**, and it is material: accelerators request 5–10%, VCs ~20% in the first round; founders trying to avoid dilution should treat an accelerator as potentially non-viable. A 2025 critique paper ("Accelerator Conundrum") argues standard 90-day cohorts **incentivise performative growth and premature dilution**, and advocates milestone-based, personalised investor introductions over "Demo Day theatre." The corporate-accelerator variant (Rio Tinto / Founders Factory, Plug and Play) is specifically valuable because it bundles **customer access with capital** — the trade a hardware company should actually want.

**Application to S5.**
- **Apply the one test to each open program:** does its customer/partner access match S5's target market (WA mining fixed-plant operators, reliability/maintenance buyers)? 
  - **Founders Factory / Rio Tinto Mining Tech — PASSES** (Rio Tinto = the buyer, Perth hub, capital + 4 months operational support). This is the accelerator to prioritise; the equity buys a customer relationship S5 cannot buy otherwise.
  - **Startmate Summer '27 (A$120k at A$1.5M post-money)** — assess on the *investor network* match, not just capital; the equity (~8%) is real dilution.
  - **Founder Institute** — low priority per the Radar; the literature's warning about generic programs that lack customer access applies.
- **Prefer programs that bundle customer access + capital over pure-cash accelerators**, because S5's binding constraint is validated demand, not cash (the R&D Tax Incentive + CSIRO Kick-Start + AC grant cover non-dilutive cash better).
- **Keep the non-dilutive lanes first:** CSIRO Kick-Start ($20–60k matched), Accelerating Commercialisation (up to $1M matched), R&D Tax Incentive — these fund the same work with **zero equity**. Use accelerator equity only to buy the customer relationship money can't otherwise open.
- **Sequence:** do not dilute before the G1/G2 pilot evidence exists — traction is what makes accelerator equity *cheap*. Gate every equity application on pilot results.

*Sources:* Cohen & Hochberg accelerator research (4–8% equity, cohort model); corporate-accelerator literature (Politecnico di Milano mapping; 4–10% for "powered-by" programs); "How to Design a Tech Startup Accelerator" (2025 — critique of the 90-day model, equity-free/virtual-first design); CBS master's thesis on accelerator participation and start-up legitimacy (equity-dilution risk); Radar §1 (Founders Factory / Rio Tinto, Startmate, CSIRO Kick-Start, AC).
