## 1. Winning public tenders — what the 2025 Commonwealth Procurement Rules actually reward

**The insight.** Updated **Commonwealth Procurement Rules (CPRs) commenced 17 Nov 2025**, replacing the July-2024 CPRs. Four changes matter to a bidder:
- **"Value for money" is now explicitly broader than price.** CPR 4.5: *"Price is not the sole factor."* Officials **must** consider quality, fitness for purpose, the supplier's **historical performance and ethical conduct**, proposal flexibility/innovation, environmental sustainability, and **whole-of-life cost** (maintenance, consumables, decommissioning).
- **Non-construction procurement threshold rose from $80,000 to $125,000** — the first increase in 20 years. Below it, agencies may proceed without open tender — **but must invite only Australian businesses.**
- **Australian-business preference is now embedded** (a departure from the old non-discrimination stance). "Australian business" = ≥50% AU ownership, AU tax resident, principal place of business in AU — **S5 qualifies**.
- **SME targets:** ≥25% of contracts by value to SMEs (≤$1bn); **40% for contracts ≤$20m**; certain panels must invite **only SMEs** below $125k.

**Application to S5.**
- The P2000 and Cataby EOIs should be written to the **non-price criteria**, not just capability: lead with **whole-of-life** framing (avoided downtime + the retainer model), **innovation** (edge-ML + DC/proof-test methodology), **local Australian SME** status (Perth, ABN), and **ethical/compliance** posture (RCM, WHS, modern-slavery-ready supply chain). Price is one column, not the decision.
- **The SME disaggregation rule is the structural lever:** CPR 5.5(d) explicitly invites agencies to *"disaggregate large projects into smaller packages."* S5's "partner the integrator, bid partial scope first" strategy is exactly aligned — say so in the EOI.
- **Historical performance is now formally weighted:** this makes the **pilot case study + evidence pack** a bidding asset, not just a sales asset. Gate-2 evidence feeds future tender scores.

*Sources:* Commonwealth Procurement Rules (2025, commenced 17 Nov 2025), Dept of Finance — value-for-money guidance CPR 4.4–4.7, 5.5–5.7; K&L Gates (Dec 2025) on Australian-business definition + SME panels; Trace Consultants (2026) on non-price evaluation reform; Dept of Finance procurement statistics 2024–25 (45,556 SME contracts, 52.4% of volume).

## 2. Qualifying Chinese suppliers — the sourcing literature's hard rules (Canton Fair T-8)

**The insight.** The academic evidence on Western firms sourcing industrial products from China converges on a few non-negotiable mechanisms:
- **Performance risk vs relational risk** must be managed together; **trust precedes control and determines whether control works** (IJPM, direct-sourcing-from-China study of 9 industrial-product firms). Trust mechanisms = prioritised customer, cross-functional team, **supplier development**, performance feedback. Control mechanisms = performance measurement, **clarity of specifications**, supplier selection, contracts.
- **The core activity is quality control, continuously** (Finnish case-study framework, 6-stage process). The recurring failure mode is not incapability but **interpretation**: suppliers treat a first order as a "draft", then quietly substitute materials once they believe the order is real. The remedy is on-site QA, clear specs, and purchase inspection.
- **A 17,000-audit study (n≈6,000 suppliers) found audits yield FEWER violations when the audit is paid for by the buyer, not the supplier** — and fewer when the team is experienced. Self-paid supplier audits are structurally unreliable.

**Application to S5.**
- The §4.2 hunt list already asks for the AU-RCM pack, price-hold/LTA terms and second sources. Add the literature's four **control** asks explicitly per vendor: **(a) a written, version-controlled specification sheet; (b) buyer-paid third-party inspection before shipment; (c) a named local-based procurement/QA contact; (d) a total-cost estimate (not unit price).**
- **Treat the first P2000-RotaMon production order as a "draft" regardless of what the vendor says** — lock materials (battery cell, accelerometer part number, MLCC second-source) into the contract and inspect on-site.
- Prefer vendors where S5 can build a **long-term relationship** (the trust mechanisms) — not the cheapest one-off quote. This is also the anti-obsolescence play given the 2026 component-price wave.

*Sources:* Zhang et al., "Supplier risk management in direct sourcing from China," *Int. J. Procurement Management* 4(4):363–385 (2011); "Developing a Framework of the Sourcing Process — Finnish Companies With Chinese Suppliers" (trust/control framework, quality-control core); Short, Toffel & Hugill, "Monitoring Global Supply Chains" (SSRN, ~17,000 audits at ~6,000 suppliers — buyer-paid audits find more violations).

## 3. The PFDavg / proof-test maths S5 is actually selling (§3-A)

**The insight.** A reliability/functional-safety buyer will test whether S5 speaks the language. The defensible arithmetic, per IEC 61511 practice:
- **Low-demand SIF approximation:** `PFDavg ≈ (λDU × TI) / 2`; rearranged for the maximum interval: **`T_max = (2 × PFDavg_target) / λDU`.**
- **Worked example (a real audit finding):** a pressure transmitter with λDU = 5×10⁻⁷/h targeting PFDavg = 1×10⁻³ (SIL 2) yields **T_max ≈ 4,000 h ≈ 5.5 months — not the 12-month default most SRS templates carry.** Continuous diagnostics that raise *diagnostic coverage (DC)* reduce λDU and legitimately extend the validated interval.
- **Proof-test coverage is the lever:** full proof test 95–99% of dangerous failures; **partial stroke test only 60–80% (valve only) — it extends the interval between full tests, it does not replace them**; smart-instrument diagnostics 70–90%. Any uncovered fraction stays in the PFDavg budget.
- **The organisational gap is the sale:** intervals sit in the SRS but the maintenance team works in a separate CMMS with **no link back to the SIL calculation** (IEC 61511-1 cl. 11.9.3 requires review as new failure data arrives). Calculated intervals typically land **3–24 months.**

**Application to S5.**
- The §3-A offer's first slide should be **the formula and the 12-month-default myth** — a "new idea the buyer doesn't have" (per the RAIN finding in Sunday's brief).
- The deliverable is concrete: **a documented FMEDA/DC argument + a calculated T_max per SIF + a proof-test-coverage table + the CMMS integration that closes the cl. 11.9.3 loop.** Never sell a "SIL-rated sensor"; sell this evidence pack.
- This is exactly the **"practical support" tool** the buyer-enablement brief called for: a **proof-test-interval & diagnostic-coverage worksheet** (λDU → PFDavg → T_max) that the reliability engineer can take to their own SRS review.

*Sources:* IEC 61511 (cl. 11.9.3, 16.2.6 partial-proof-test credit); exida PFDavg fundamentals; ifluids *IEC 61511 Proof Testing Intervals* (worked T_max example, coverage table); midstreamcalculator SIL-verification fundamentals (1oo1/1oo2/2oo3 equations, DC effect); beefed.ai SIS proof-testing KPIs (PFDavg trend as the best integrity indicator).

## 4. TinyML on-device acoustic anomaly detection — evidence for RotaMon's edge-ML claim

**The insight.** The peer-reviewed edge-ML literature now supports exactly what RotaMon claims. A 2026 TinyML pipeline that extracts **Mel-Frequency Cepstral Coefficients (MFCC)** and trains a lightweight classifier for **acoustic anomaly detection on microcontrollers** reports **91% test accuracy / balanced F1 = 0.91** on the UrbanSound8K benchmark, with early stopping at 36 epochs — feasible on ESP32-class silicon. **DCASE 2024 Task 2** provides the industrial benchmark: *"first-shot unsupervised anomalous sound detection for machine condition monitoring."* Research explicitly frames the trade-off S5 must price: **maximising recall in safety-critical settings** (a missed anomaly costs more than a false alarm).

**Application to S5.**

## 5. Global e-commerce operations — the honest reality for §3-E

**The insight.** The cross-border fulfilment literature is clear on *model choice by stage*, and the B2B-marketplace data is a reality check:
- **Cross-border (ship-from-China) fulfilment suits testing a market** — start selling quickly, minimal overhead — while **in-country 3PL** suits scaling and speed. A good 3PL mirrors what works in the core market into the new one.
- **The industrial-marketplace ceiling is real.** BCG's analysis of Amazon Business found the **vast majority of its revenue is standardised, high-velocity categories** (office, janitorial, electrical); in UK MRO distribution **~70% of the assortment was unavailable on Amazon**, and where it was, **prices averaged ~30% higher than a local B2B distributor with ~one-week delivery.** Complex/service-heavy industrial categories resist pure e-commerce.

**Application to S5.**
- This validates **§3-E's stance**: kits/packs via **Chinese 3PLs with kitting support** for low-volume market testing, with **narrow margins accepted as the price of demand-signal data** — not as a profit centre. Cross-border first, in-country 3PL only once a SKU proves repeat demand.
- **Do not expect the marketplace to carry the safety-grade B2B offer.** The §3-A/B consultancy + hardware belongs in the tender/direct channel (§1, §7); §3-E is a separate, consumer/SMB, margin-testing lane. Keeping them apart protects the premium positioning.
- Any consumer smart device in a pack must still carry the **AU Smart Device Security statement of compliance** (in force 4 Mar 2026) — a hard gate before listing.

*Sources:* Passport Global, *Cross-Border vs In-Country Fulfillment*; Extensiv, *Cross-Border Fulfillment*; Sciencedirect, "Logistics service sharing in cross-border e-commerce" (2024); BCG, *B2B Distributors Can Survive the Age of Amazon* (Amazon Business ~$25bn gross; ~70% of UK MRO assortment absent; ~30% price premium, ~1-week delivery).
