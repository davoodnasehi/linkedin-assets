## 5. Jobs to Be Done / Outcome-Driven Innovation — 86% success claim is practitioner-only; ODI's algorithm critiqued as "technically and intellectually flawed" (product decisions, §3-C, §3-F)

**The insight.** JTBD theory (Ulwick 1990, Christensen 2003) claims products designed around JTBD research have an **86% new-product success rate vs ~17% for traditional development** (Strategyn/ODI research, 2016) and that **95% of new products fail because companies focus on the customer rather than the job** (Christensen HBS analysis, 2016). However, **most published evidence consists of case studies and method papers from the firms that sell it** (Strategyn, Rework). **No controlled tests exist**. A 2018 paper (Lucassen et al.) noted job stories had "not been studied in research yet." Two incompatible schools exist: **Christensen/Moesta (qualitative, interview-driven "Switch" method)** and **Ulwick (quantitative ODI, 50–150 desired-outcome statements per job, importance/satisfaction survey, Opportunity Algorithm)**. **Critiques of ODI's mathematical foundations are explicit**: Gerry Katz and Jeffery Pinegar argue Ulwick's Opportunity Algorithm is *"technically and intellectually flawed"* (Research.mental-momentum.ai, *Comparison of ODI and JTBD*, 2026). From behavioural economics, the Moesta/Christensen school argues ODI's extreme focus on functional execution ignores fundamental irrationality of human behaviour. The primary limitation of Christensen's JTBD: **abstraction and lack of a rigid, step-by-step methodology suitable for engineering execution** — enterprise implementations stall due to cross-functional misalignment.

**Application to S5.**
- **Do not adopt ODI/JTBD as the product-decision framework for §3-C (energy/BESS/EV telemetry SKU) or §3-F (fabrication workshop).** The 86% figure is vendor-published, not independently replicated. The two schools are incompatible — mixing them creates conceptual confusion.
- **Use the Radar's existing validation machine instead:** the §3-A/§3-B field_engineer validation notes (live operator pain, fatality/prosecution signals, hiring surges, M&A integration triggers) are *observed jobs in context* — superior to abstract job maps. The "category entry points" from the 10-21 brief (fatality, MSMS audit, conveyor fire, M&A integration, reliability engineer job ad, new plant commissioning, insurer renewal) are the CEPs buyers actually enter the market through.
- **If a structured method is needed, use the ISO 14224 taxonomy + SAE JA1011 seven questions** (10-22 brief) — these are standards the buyer is *certified in*, not a vendor framework. They map directly to the data exchange format the buyer's CMMS already expects.
- **Owner time: 0 minutes.** This is a "do not do" brief — it saves Dav from evaluating a practitioner framework with thin evidence.

*Sources:* Rework "Jobs to Be Done (JTBD): Framework, Examples, and How to Apply It" (2016 Strategyn/ODI 86% claim, Christensen 95% failure claim); Pushers "Jobs to be done (JTBD): the theory, two schools, examples" (2018 Lucassen "not studied in research yet", ODI 50–150 outcomes); KnowledgeLib.io "Jobs-to-Be-Done 2026" (two schools, constraints, qualitative hard to scale); Mental Momentum Research "Comparison of ODI and JTBD" (2026, Katz & Pinegar "technically and intellectually flawed", behavioural economics critique); Tandfonline "Jobs-to-be-done meets mobile banking" (2026, functional + non-functional factors).
